# Cultural Provenance Is Not Yield-Bearing RWA

NanoClaw studies RWA infrastructure because tokenized funds, custody systems,
agentic payments, and programmable collateral are shaping the language of
digital assets. That does not mean every tokenized cultural object should be
described as collateral, yield, or an investment product.

## The Distinction

| Category | Cultural provenance | Yield-bearing RWA collateral |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary question | What is this object, edition, record, or publication action? | What financial exposure or collateral value does this token represent? |
| Useful proof | Catalog record, rights review, edition status, publication receipt. | Custody, eligibility, fund terms, yield rules, collateral rules. |
| NanoClaw role | Document reviewable provenance and human approvals. | Research only; no product claim. |
| Risk if blurred | Creates false expectations about ownership, scarcity, resale, or rights. | Creates financial, regulatory, and custody expectations. |

## Why NanoClaw Tracks RWA

RWA systems are useful references for discipline:

- precise terms;
- clear eligibility boundaries;
- audit-friendly APIs;
- custody and transfer language;
- human approval around irreversible actions.

NanoClaw can borrow that discipline without borrowing the regulated claim.

## Appropriate Language

Use:

- provenance receipt;
- edition record;
- publication status;
- rights review;
- collector preview;
- public-safe metadata;
- human-approved action.

Avoid unless a full legal structure exists:

- yield;
- collateral;
- security;
- fund;
- guaranteed value;
- investment return;
- custody service;
- regulated product.

## Practical Rule

For cultural objects, NanoClaw should publish metadata and receipts that answer:

1. What was reviewed?
2. What was approved?
3. What was published?
4. What remains private?
5. Which agent action stopped for human approval?

That is already valuable. It does not need to pretend to be a financial product.
